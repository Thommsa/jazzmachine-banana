A Dutch Big Band Settles Its Name, Mostly

After a decade of “New Sound Jazz Machine,” the ensemble in Hengelo has dropped the “new,” kept the machine, and acquired a parade dragon with a red herring tail.

By The East Sound Desk
Hengelo, the Netherlands · Oct. 21, 2026

The group chat, as these things go, began with a rehearsal schedule and ended with a shortlist. Dirk suggested Twents Jazz Orkest. Pieter, on trombone, offered Het Tor dansorkest. Gijs, on trumpet, went for Sint Joris en zijn Draken. Igor proposed Tukker swing. At 7:02 a.m., Joost sent “New name JM.” Later, Pieter returned with Swingersgroep de machines.

None of them stuck. The band is still called East Sound Jazz Machine — the name the director, Joris Bolhaar, had favored, minus the “new” that had become phonetically awkward in Dutch and factually inaccurate for a group older than ten years.

“Cool was yesterday. East is today. And hot is what remains after too long on the bus to Twente.”

The logo is a Chinese street-parade dragon: one creature, several pairs of human legs in black shoes, a bright red herring for a tail. The gears that once defined the machine have been retired. The herring is not a fish-market accident; it is a nod to a song title and to the band’s habit of not taking itself entirely seriously.

The New Cool Collective has not changed its name to East Hot Collective. That rumor, which originated in the same building as the merch page, is false.

Merch now exists, of a kind. A patch, a shirt, a sticker, and a few shirts that print the rejected names as if they had won. Checkout does not ship. The treasurer has not yet bought east.sound.jazzmachine.band. The postman, according to the store, plays bass.

Asked for comment, the machine’s voicemail said: leave a message after the red herring.
